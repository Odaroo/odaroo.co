Front page

Six cartridges.
One maker.
Nothing to install.

Odaroo & Co. is a maker's label run like a small studio. Every game in this paper is an original, and every one of them loads in the tab you are reading right now.

Welcome to the paper. Around here a release is a cartridge, and there are six on the rack: a 1930s cartoon card rumble, a dream-wind platformer, a lab you walk into with a headset on, a river of painted tiles, a neon-noir one-hit-kill, and a blade puzzle that needs both of your hands and possibly a friend.

Nothing needs a download, a launcher or an account. Pick a cartridge, press PLAY, and it starts. Phones, desktops, controllers and VR headsets are all welcome.

This issue opens with the scene (the videos, the stream, the music and the Discord) and then walks the whole rack, one cartridge at a time. Scroll slowly. The cartridges turn as you go, and the bar on top jumps anywhere.

Inkville

A rubberhose rumble. Collect the acts, play the arcade, battle online.

It is the 1930s and the cartoon is made of ink. Inkville's stars are tabletop pieces come to life: chess men, checkers, playing cards, dice, poker chips and pool balls, collected as cards and sent out to rumble.

Around the card game sits a whole arcade. Eight games share one ladder: Tetr-Ink, Ice-Ink, Disc-Ink, Panel-Ink, Ink-Put!, Dance Dance Ink, Ink-Tar Hero and Lumen Ink, with the OCTA-THON locked in the vault until everything else is open. Win cups to unlock the next game, earn XP to level your Inklet, then take it online.

Every drawing and every note of music is generated in code.

Lunatide

A dream-wind platformer under a tidal moon.

Binky runs, jumps and flutters through a moonlit dream with a Wind Ring in hand. Fire the ring to catch a creature, carry it, throw it, or spring off it for a double jump.

Five Visions are played in order: the quay of Moonlit Harbor, the Moonpetal Garden, the two-world Looking-Glass, a board run down Moonwake Slide and a boss in the Jester's Ring. Each stage hides 150 dream stones and six Moon Shards.

Find everything and a sixth Vision rises on the world map. It also plays offline, and in a headset the whole dream wraps around you.

VR Labs

Put on a headset: a lab of VR games you pick up with your hands.

One page, one headset, three experiments. The Lab is a room you stand in. Pick up a cartridge, push it into the console, and the room fades into a game.

Moonrun is a first-person dream run from Lunatide: rooftops, wall-runs and a ghost of your best time. Moonstone Toss is a throwing gallery under the pines. Glove Range hands you gravity gloves: point, hold the grip and flick, and the target flies to your palm.

Home is always on your left wrist.

Lusolaos

A river of painted tiles, from the Atlantic to the Mekong. Turn, swap, breathe.

Tap a tile to turn it, drag one to swap it, and lead the water from the spring to the sea. Twelve named stages alternate between the Açores and Laos, painted in cobalt on tin-white like the tiles of São Miguel.

There are no stars, no par and no move counter. A stage is simply complete. A new river arrives every day, and an endless one waits after the last stage.

The sound comes in four layers with a switch each: music, nature, effects and binaural beats in three tunings. In Settings, Immersive hangs the wall in front of you in VR, or over your own room in AR.

Chrome Animals

A neon-noir massacre. One hit kills. Pick a mask.

One hit kills, both ways. Four story chapters climb through a neon city to Dr. Halcyon. Night Shift is a ten-minute arcade run on a daily seed. The level editor turns your own floor into a share code.

Every line of English carries its Japanese underneath. The twelve-song soundtrack has its own player on the title menu, CHROME AMP. All of the art and all of the music are generated in code.

Edgework 刃渡り

Eight nights of thin blades over a red seal. Never touch an edge.

Every night hides a red seal under a pile of spring-loaded blades. Slide a blade by its blue grip and hold it there. Let go and it snaps home. Press the seal to clear the night.

Touch an edge and you are cut. The night starts over, and the third cut of a run is game over, with no retry. All eight nights are one run against the clock, ranked on a worldwide board.

The holds add up: one blade on the first night, four on the last, plus the finger that presses. Bring both hands, or a friend.
